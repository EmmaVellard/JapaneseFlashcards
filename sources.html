About the decks

A clearer picture of what you study.

Where the words come from, what the levels mean, and how to read the cards.

Study lists, with room for nuance.

The JLPT does not publish an official exhaustive vocabulary or kanji syllabus. These N5 and N4 labels are community study guidance, not a guarantee of exam coverage.

The current decks contain 84 N5 kanji, 169 N4 kanji, 681 N5 vocabulary cards, and 638 N4 vocabulary cards. Vocabulary includes 15 supplementary cards, including useful words already in this app and separate readings for 一日 and 私.

Kanji decks contain individual characters. Compounds such as 月曜日 belong to vocabulary. A word can have more than one reading, so cards for 開く【あく】 and 開く【ひらく】 are kept separately. Bare reading quizzes skip ambiguous homographs.

Reading the readings.

On’yomi are written in katakana, and kun’yomi in hiragana. A dot marks where okurigana begin: 食 is た.べる in 食べる. A leading or trailing hyphen marks suffix or prefix use. A dash on its own means no reading is listed in that category.

These are dictionary readings, including some uncommon ones. A kanji’s reading in an actual word depends on that word; not every dictionary reading is equally useful at N5 or N4.

A word in context.

Each card has a Japanese example sentence and an English translation. Sentences come from Tatoeba, via OpenJLPT, or were written for this app. Tatoeba examples link to their original sentence and contributor attribution, and are used under CC BY 2.0 FR. Original learner examples are shared under CC BY-SA 4.0.

Kanji examples show the character used in a sentence. Sentence grammar can be more advanced than the word’s study level. Examples appear after the answer is revealed, so they do not give away the question.

Sources and corrections.

The baseline is OpenJLPT, using Jonathan Waller’s community level lists, JMdict and KANJIDIC2. Dictionary data are copyright the Electronic Dictionary Research and Development Group (EDRDG), used under its licence.

The app includes local corrections to the source, including せっけん (soap), 半分 (half), the inclusive meanings of 以下 and 以上, and the two readings of 一日. Questionable translations were checked against dictionary references including Cambridge’s Japanese–English dictionary.
